1 · How it works

The terminal decides, the bot only executes

The bot has no opinion of its own. The BTC Prediction Machine (the BTC Machine tab here, and the same terminal inside the kit) watches every 15-minute and 5-minute “Bitcoin Up or Down” round on Polymarket. When its model sees value, it issues a call: which side to buy, at what price and for how much. The bot listens to the terminal and places the same order on your Polymarket account, usually within a few hundred milliseconds. It follows the rounds you tick in the CALLS box: 5-minute, 15-minute or both. We run it on the 5-minute rounds.

  1. Terminal · 4× a second1Live dataBTC price, trades and futures from Binance; the Chainlink price the round settles on; Polymarket’s order book for UP and DOWN.
  2. Terminal2ForecastThe chance that UP or DOWN wins: distance to the strike against volatility and time left, tilted by 16 metrics and corrected by the model’s own track record.
  3. Terminal3CallIf the chance beats the price by your edge threshold: one call per round, for example BUY UP @ 51¢ · $10.08
  4. Your computer4BotSees the call on the terminal’s live stream at once and sends a market order capped at the call price plus slippage.
  5. Polymarket5ResultThe round settles on the official Chainlink price. A winning share pays $1; with auto-redeem on, the money returns to your cash by itself.

What a call is based on

The forecast
  • Distance vs noise. A round settles on the Chainlink 60-second average at the close against the “price to beat” fixed at the start. The core of the model is how far BTC is from that strike, how many minutes are left and how volatile BTC is right now.
  • 16 metrics tilt the odds early in the round and fade out near the end:
    GapGap velocityRSIMACDBook imbalanceTaker flowStoch RSI4-hour trendOpen interestTop traders L/SGlobal L/SFundingMean reversion3 candlesMomentumVWAP
  • Context. How this time slot has resolved over the past year, the news calendar (FOMC, CPI, jobs) and whether volatility is normal for the hour.
  • Calibration. The terminal scores its own forecasts and shrinks them when it has been over-confident. It never makes them bolder.
A call needs all of these
  • The entry window. 15-minute rounds: from 10:00 to 2:00 before the close; 5-minute rounds: from 4:00 to 0:45. At most one call per round.
  • Value. Expected profit per $1 of at least your edge threshold (default 5%): model chance ÷ price − 1.
  • Price. The side costs no more than your cap (default 55¢).
  • Something to buy. At least 10 shares on offer at that price and an order book no older than 15 seconds. Otherwise the terminal shows “edge, but nothing to buy” and waits.
  • Protection not tripped. 6 losses in a row pause the calls for 15 minutes; −25% from the peak or −30% in a day stops them until you resume.

What the bot does and does not do

It does
  • Buy every new call once, for the markets ticked in CALLS (5M and/or 15M).
  • Pay at most the call price plus slippage: +9¢ under 20¢, +5¢ from 20 to 39¢, +2¢ from 40¢.
  • Spend the call’s stake, but never more than your limit (MAX_STAKE) and never less than the exchange minimum of 5 shares.
  • Retry every 0.25 s for up to 3 s if nothing is on offer at its cap, then give up. No fill means no money spent.
  • Load each new round’s market in advance and keep its connection to the exchange warm, to save time on the order.
  • Reconnect by itself when the terminal restarts. The start script restarts the bot if it crashes.
It does not
  • Pick sides or change settings: it only follows the terminal.
  • Sell early or use a stop-loss: every position is held until the round settles.
  • Trade while the terminal is closed or the computer is asleep.
  • Act on calls older than 60 seconds, e.g. after a restart.
  • Send your private key anywhere. It stays in a file on your computer and signs the orders locally, the way MetaMask does.
  • Guarantee a profit. Read the warning before you start.
2 · The CALLS settings

Everything the bot buys is decided in this box

The CALLS box sits at the bottom of FORECAST in the BTC Prediction Machine: the BTC Machine tab of this site shows it, and the kit’s own copy of the terminal on your computer has the same box, which is the one the bot listens to. The bot copies its calls one to one and only adds your limit per bet and its slippage. Hover a number to find it on the picture.

  1. 1
    ON

    The session is running. When it is off (or stopped), there are no calls and the bot simply waits.

  2. 2
    Budget $

    The balance the calls are sized from. Set it to your real cash on Polymarket, so that a 1% call means 1% of your real money.

    your Polymarket cash · refresh daily
  3. 3
    size

    auto: quarter-Kelly of the edge, scaled by time, session, drawdown and recent form; 1–6% of the balance per call. fixed: always the base %.

    auto
  4. 4
    base %

    The stake for fixed sizing. Greyed out in auto mode.

  5. 5
    after a loss

    same stake means no chasing. recover and ×2 raise the stake after each loss to win the series back; with real money and missed fills that can empty an account quickly.

    same stake not recover / ×2
  6. 6
    can’t cover

    When the balance cannot pay the next step of a series: write the series off and start again, or bet what is left.

    write off, restart
  7. 7
    edge ≥

    Minimum value for a call, as expected profit per $1: model chance ÷ price − 1. In the picture: 0.61 ÷ 0.51 − 1 = +19%.

    5 or more
  8. 8
    price ≤

    The most a side may cost. Above about 55¢ the terminal’s own record shows no edge.

    55
  9. 9
    15M / 5M

    Which rounds get calls, and so which rounds the bot buys: 5-minute, 15-minute or both.

    5M on (what we run) · 15M optional
  10. 10
    restart with new budget · stop · apply

    apply changes the rules without touching the balance. restart starts a new session from the Budget; the history is kept. stop ends the calls.

  11. 11
    The call

    BUY YES · UP @ 51¢ for $10.08: the side, the price and the stake. “valid until 0:45 left” is the end of the entry window (5-minute rounds: 0:45 left, 15-minute: 2:00); then the number of shares and what you win or lose. “step 1” is the first bet of a series.

  12. 12
    How the stake was sized

    The model said 61%, calibration trimmed it to 59%. Kelly 16% × 0.25 (quarter-Kelly) × time 0.73 × session 1 × drawdown 0.4 (the balance was 15% below its peak) × form 1 = 1.1% of the balance.

  13. 13
    Session line

    Paper balance, its change and peak, the current base %, the drawdown factor, recent form and wins / losses. Paper means every call counted as filled at its price, without fees.

3 · Accuracy and history

How to judge the calls before you risk money

FORECAST ACCURACY sits at the bottom-left of the BTC Prediction Machine. It is the model’s report card and the record of every call. Check it before you switch the bot to real money, and every day after.

  1. 1
    MODEL · hit rate

    How often the side the model favoured went on to win, stamped at fixed checkpoints (15M: 12, 10, 7, 5, 3, 2, 1 minutes left; 5M: 4, 3, 2, 1, 0.5). The Brier score is the error of the probabilities: lower is better, 0.25 is a coin flip.

  2. 2
    MARKET · same moments

    How often the market’s own favourite (the side priced above 50¢) won at the same moments. Here both are 82%: the model is about as good as the crowd, so any profit has to come from the moments when they disagree.

  3. 3
    CALLS · balance

    The paper account of the calls: starting budget, profit or loss, wins and losses. Every call is counted as filled at its price, with no fees.

  4. 4
    BY CONFIDENCE

    Calibration. Forecasts are grouped by confidence, and a group that claims 77% should win about 77%. When a group wins less than it claims, the terminal trusts it less and bets smaller.

  5. 5
    BY TIME LEFT

    Accuracy by minutes left: 66% with 4 minutes to go, 94% at 30 seconds. Late forecasts are right more often, but by then the favourite is expensive and there is little left to earn.

  6. 6
    CALL HISTORY

    Every call: time, round, side @ price, stake (share of the balance), result and the balance after. all history opens every session, with a CSV export.

  7. 7
    P&L per $1 by price

    Where the calls make or lose money. “won 38% vs needs 43%”: sides bought at 43¢ on average must win more than 43% of the time to profit, and these won 38%.

Judge the calls by P&L, not by the hit rate. A call pays when it wins more often than its price implies: a side bought at 51¢ has to win more than 51% of the time. That is why CALLS · balance and P&L per $1 matter more than the big percentages at the top, and why a few hundred rounds say more than one evening.
4 · Bot settings, log and speed

Seven settings, two of which you choose at start

The start script asks for the two that matter: test or real money, and the limit per bet. The rest have tested defaults. To change one, add a line such as SLIP_HIGH=0.03 to the .env file in the bot folder and restart the bot.

SettingDefaultWhat it does
DRY_RUN11 is test mode: the bot prints what it would buy and sends nothing. 0 sends real orders. The start script sets it: option 1 is the test, option 2 is real money.
MAX_STAKE5The most one call may spend, in dollars (USDC). The bot takes the call’s stake and cuts it to this limit, but never goes below the exchange minimum of 5 shares, counted at its price cap (about $2.65 for a call at 51¢). The start script asks for it in real mode.
SLIP_LOW0.09For calls priced under 20¢: pay up to 9¢ more than the call price. Cheap sides move in big steps and still leave room to pay up.
SLIP_MID0.05For calls at 20–39¢: up to 5¢ more.
SLIP_HIGH0.02For calls at 40¢ and above: up to 2¢ more. Here every extra cent eats most of the edge.
RETRY_MS3000If nothing is on offer at the cap, keep trying for this many milliseconds: the price sometimes comes back.
RETRY_GAP250The pause between tries, in milliseconds.
More slippage buys more calls but at worse prices. On a call at 51¢ the whole edge is often a few cents, so paying 5¢ more can turn a good call into a bad one.

Prefer typing the command yourself? In the bot folder: DRY_RUN=0 MAX_STAKE=20 node bot.mjs in Terminal$env:DRY_RUN='0'; $env:MAX_STAKE='20'; node bot.mjs in PowerShell. The start script runs exactly this for you after “2 → limit → YES”, and on top keeps the computer awake and restarts the bot if it crashes.

Faster fills: run it on a server near London optional, for advanced users

Most misses come from distance: the order book is in London, and an order from home needs 150–450 ms there and back. A small rented server (a VPS) close to London cuts that to about 15–35 ms, so the bot catches noticeably more calls.

What and where
  • Any small Linux VPS with 1 GB of memory is enough, about $5 a month.
  • Close to London, in a country Polymarket does not restrict, for example Sweden (Stockholm), Spain (Madrid) or Switzerland (Zurich).
  • Not London, Dublin, Amsterdam, Frankfurt or Paris: the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, Germany and France are on Polymarket’s restricted list (help.polymarket.com → Geographic restrictions).
How
  • Install Node.js 24+ on the server and copy 1-Trenchlight and 2-polybot there (your filled-in .env included).
  • Start the terminal with npm start in 1-Trenchlight and the bot with DRY_RUN=0 MAX_STAKE=20 node bot.mjs in 2-polybot, each in its own tmux window so they keep running after you disconnect.
  • Open the site from your computer through an SSH tunnel: ssh -L 8787:localhost:8787 user@server, then http://localhost:8787/trenchlight.
What it does not change: the market makers next to the exchange stay faster, so the bot catches more calls, not all of them. And a server does not change where you may trade: Polymarket’s rules follow where you live, and using servers or VPNs to get around them breaks its terms.
5 · Setup, step by step

From zero to a running bot

About 20 minutes the first time. You need a Mac or a Windows PC that can stay on, an internet connection, a MetaMask wallet and a Polymarket account with some USDC. Steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 are done once; after that you only start the terminal and the bot.

Steps for macOS.
  1. Install Node.js once
    1. Open nodejs.org and download the LTS version (24 or newer).
    2. Open the downloaded .pkg and click Continue until the installation finishes.
    3. Run the downloaded .msi installer and keep the default options. Skipped this step? The kit’s scripts notice it and offer to install Node.js for you.

    Why it is needed: the terminal and the bot are programs written in JavaScript. cmd and PowerShell only give them a windowThe Terminal app only gives them a window; Node.js is what actually runs them, the way a video needs a player. It is free and takes a minute.

  2. Download and unpack the kit once
    1. Download the kit for your system at the bottom of this page.
    2. Double-click Trenchlight-kit-mac.zip. You get a folder Trenchlight-kit-mac; move it to Documents.
    3. Lift macOS’s block on downloaded scripts for the whole folder: open Terminal (Cmd+Space, type Terminal, Enter), type the command below with a space at the end, drag the folder into the window and press Enter.
      xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine
      If macOS already showed “Apple could not verify…”, click Done (not Move to Bin) and run the command.
    4. Right-click Trenchlight-kit-windows.zip → Properties → tick Unblock at the bottom → OK. This stops Windows from blocking the scripts later.
    5. Right-click the zip again → Extract All… → choose Documents → Extract. If Windows ever says “Windows protected your PC”, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Start the terminal
    1. In 1-Trenchlight, double-click trenchlight.commandtrenchlight.bat.
    2. A window with text opens: that is the server. Keep it open while you use the site and the bot.
    3. The browser opens http://localhost:8787/trenchlight by itself. The first start downloads a year of BTC candles and takes a few minutes.
  4. Prepare Polymarket once
    1. Install MetaMask in your browser and create a wallet. Write the 12-word secret phrase on paper and keep it offline; the bot never needs it. A separate MetaMask account just for trading is best.
    2. On polymarket.com: Log in → MetaMask, then Deposit USDC.
    3. Settings → Trading → turn on Auto-redeem wins, so winnings return to your cash by themselves.
    4. Place one small bet by hand ($1). On the first bet Polymarket asks you to enable trading and sign in MetaMask; after that the bot can trade too.
    5. Copy your Polymarket wallet address: avatar (top right) → your profile → the 0x… address under your name. It is not your MetaMask address and not a deposit address from the Deposit window.
    6. Get the private key of that MetaMask account: MetaMask → ⋮ → Account details → Show private key → password. It is 64 characters of 0–9 and a–f, not the 12 words.
  5. Put your address and key into the bot once

    The 2-polybot folder has a file called .env. In the download it is a template: placeholders instead of an address and a key, nobody’s wallet.

    1. Double-click 2-polybot/1-setup-bot.command2-polybot\1-setup-bot.bat.
    2. It installs the bot’s libraries (1–2 minutes), then asks for the address and the key and writes them into .env on your computer. The key is not shown while you paste it (Cmd+VCtrl+V or right-click); that is normal.
    3. Made a typo? Run it again and answer y to enter both anew.

    By hand instead: open 2-polybot in Finder, press Cmd+Shift+. to show hidden files and open .env with TextEdit.open 2-polybot\.env with Notepad. Replace the two PASTE_… values with yours, change nothing else and save. Run 1-setup-bot once anyway: it installs the libraries.

  6. Turn on the calls in the terminal

    In the kit’s copy of the terminal (the browser page that opened in step 3), open the BTC Prediction Machine tab. The CALLS box is at the bottom of FORECAST (right column). Set:

    Budget $
    your cash on Polymarket
    size
    auto (Kelly × edge · time · history)
    after a loss
    same stake (no chasing)
    can’t cover
    write off, restart
    edge ≥
    5 %
    price ≤
    55 ¢
    markets
    5M ticked (what we run); tick 15M too if you want the bot to follow the 15-minute calls as well

    Press OK — start. ON lights up. Calls come only in rounds where the model sees value, not in every round.

  7. Test without money
    1. Double-click 2-polybot/2-start-bot.command2-polybot\2-start-bot.bat and choose 1.
    2. Watch for 20–30 minutes: every call in the terminal should show up in the bot window as a call #… line. Nothing is bought in test mode.
    3. Stop it with Ctrl+C.
  8. Real money
    1. Start 2-start-bot again, choose 2, enter the limit per bet (Enter = $5) and type YES. That is all: the script starts the bot with real orders for you, there is no command to type.
    2. Keep both windows open and the computer awake: plugged in, lid open. While the bot runs, the start script keeps the computer from sleeping. If you must close the lid: Control Panel → Power Options → “Choose what closing the lid does” → Do nothing.
    3. Once a day, press restart with new budget in CALLS and enter your real cash, then compare the real balance with the paper one. The difference is missed calls and fees.
Stop and start again. Stop the bot with Ctrl+C in its window (or close the window); stop the terminal the same way. Next time: start the terminal, wait for the browser, then start the bot → 2 → limit → YES. The call session and its history are kept.

Read before you start

Why real profit is smaller than on paper

On paper the calls often look like easy money: on good days the paper balance grows several times over, because it counts every call as bought at the exact price on screen. With real money the result is much smaller, because the bot often cannot buy at that price. Two reasons:

  1. 1
    Your bot is farther from the exchange than the fastest traders

    Polymarket’s order book runs on servers in London. Market makers and professional bots rent servers right next to it, so their orders arrive within milliseconds. Your bot runs on a home computer, and every order travels through your Wi-Fi and your provider to London and back: often 150–450 ms. When Bitcoin jumps, market makers reprice at once and faster bots take the cheap shares first, so by the time your order arrives the price you saw is often gone.

    Bots next to the exchangea few ms
    A rented server near London~15–35 ms
    Your home computer150–450 ms

    Faster: rent a small server (a VPS, about $5 a month) close to London and run the terminal and the bot there; the bot then catches noticeably more calls. How →

  2. 2
    At that moment there is not enough to buy

    These rounds have thin order books, the 5-minute ones especially: at the call’s price there may be only a handful of shares, sometimes fewer than your stake. The bot never pays more than its price cap, so if nobody sells at or below it, nothing is bought, and if only part is on offer, you get part. Nights, weekends and the last minute of a round are the thinnest.

  • Why the gap. The calls you miss tend to be the better ones (the price runs away mostly when faster traders agree with the call), and the paper balance ignores Polymarket’s taker fee: roughly 3–7% of the stake, about 3.5% at 50¢.
  • Start in test mode, then with a small limit, and use only money you can afford to lose. Polymarket is closed in a number of countries, among them Russia, the UK, the US, Germany and France; using a VPN to get around that breaks its rules. Not financial advice.
